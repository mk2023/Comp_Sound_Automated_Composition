Using Pitch Set Theory for Automated Composition

Building this project helped me concretely understand pitch-set operations. In class, I could follow Tn, I, and R as more of a mathematical concept, as it was introduced in linear algebra. However, physically implementing them made me understand how they work in a more musical way.

When I first encountered this concept during the lecture, at first glance, I thought randomizing the orders would produce random notes without an audible theme. However, while creating the webpage, inevitably, I had to test the operations repeatedly to check what would happen. Through this process, I discovered that pitch-set composition revealed a hidden order. Instead of making repetitive melodies or chord progressions, it was instead reusing a same set of notes in different forms through transposing, inverting, and reordering. Consequently, the resulting music/tune contained a sense of musical consistency, even without a traditional tonal framework.

From a Web Audio perspective, I found that implementation details were significant. I implemented playback with an AudioContext, one oscillator per note, and a gain envelope for each. I had to use short exponential ramps to avoid the clicks between notes, which did not sound appealing.

Overall, I realized that pitch becomes a modular building block in musical composition. In visual art, it is similar to working with a color palette. Although the surface of the music may change, the underlying structure is similar. Thus, the result becomes familiar as one listens to it, yet it maintains a hint of freshness in each new phrase.
I would not have been able to see the significance of pitch-set theory in music composition if I had only examined an array of numbers going through an arithmetic operation. Through this project, I now understand pitch-set automation less as “random notes” and more as a controlled transformation system where a cohesive musical theme emerges.
